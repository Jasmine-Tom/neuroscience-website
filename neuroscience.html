The neuron

A neuron is an excitable cell specialized for communication. Different neuron types have different shapes and roles.

🌿 Dendrites Receive many incoming signals.
🟣 Soma / cell body Contains the nucleus and integrates cellular activity.
⚡ Axon Conducts electrical signals away from the cell body.
🧤 Myelin Insulating material around many axons that can increase conduction speed.
🔵 Axon terminal Communicates with another cell across a synapse.
Think visually:

Dendrites are the “branches,” the soma is the “hub,” the axon is the “cable,” and terminals are the “outgoing connection points.”

Time travel

The story of neuroscience.

Neuroscience did not appear overnight. It grew through anatomy, microscopy, physiology, electrical recording, imaging and modern molecular tools.

🏺
ANTIQUITY

Early ideas about the brain

Ancient physicians and philosophers debated whether the brain, heart or other organs played central roles in sensation, thought and behavior.

Why it mattered: Neuroscience began with a fundamental question: where does sensation and thought come from?

🔬
19TH–20TH CENTURY

Santiago Ramón y Cajal

Cajal's detailed drawings and microscopy helped establish the neuron doctrine: nervous tissue is composed of individual cells rather than one continuous web.

Visual idea: Think of the brain as a gigantic forest of specialized cells rather than a single blob.

🎨
1890s–1900s

Neurons become visible

Histological staining and microscopy allowed researchers to visualize increasingly detailed structures of nervous tissue.

Why it mattered: Structure could now be connected to questions about organization and function.

⚡
20TH CENTURY

Electrical signaling

Electrophysiology transformed neuroscience by allowing scientists to measure electrical activity in neurons and nerves.

Big idea: neurons communicate using electrical changes across their membranes and chemical/electrical signaling at synapses.

🧲
🧠
LATE 20TH CENTURY

Brain imaging

Techniques such as CT, MRI, PET and functional MRI gave researchers increasingly powerful ways to examine living brains.

Why it mattered: scientists could study brain structure and, with different techniques, aspects of activity without needing to observe the brain directly.

🧬
🧬
21ST CENTURY

Connectomes, genetics & modern neuroscience

Researchers combine genetics, microscopy, computational modeling, large-scale neural recordings and increasingly detailed maps of neural connections.

The new question: how do enormous networks of individual cells create perception, memory, behavior and thought?
